How We Work

From a standalone technical project to ongoing customer and community operations, we learn the product, agree on the scope, and take responsibility for the work.

The project process

Small projects start at $149 for a defined task. The free initial assessment establishes fit, scope, deliverables, and price. Complex dashboards and larger implementations receive custom quotes.

  1. 1. Describe the problem

    Tell us what should happen, what happens instead, and what a useful result looks like. For data work, describe the sources and the question you want to answer. A general description is enough to begin.

  2. 2. Assess and agree

    We review the request and agree on the output, boundaries, price, and any access needed. If deeper investigation is required, we can quote a paid diagnostic that delivers written findings and next steps. A fix is included only when agreed.

  3. 3. Deliver the work

    We carry out the agreed task and check it against the expected result. If investigation reveals a larger project, we agree on a revised scope and price before continuing.

  4. 4. Review and hand over

    We review the deliverable with you, explain how to use it, and document the changes, remaining limitations, and any recommended follow-up.

Support improvements can begin with a defined review or improvement project. Continuing operational responsibility starts with a discovery conversation and a custom monthly proposal. Standalone projects require no long-term commitment.

Support principles

For customer support and community operations, these principles guide how we investigate issues and work with your team.

01

Learn the business

DA operators should understand the product rather than simply memorize scripts. A scripted response can answer a question. Understanding the product means being able to solve the problem behind it. Every engagement starts with a learning phase: we study the product, the people who use it, and the problems they actually run into before handling a single conversation.

02

Build the knowledge layer

Before operators can resolve problems consistently, the knowledge has to be organized. That means documentation, recurring case summaries, policy references, troubleshooting flows, internal escalation guides, and known product issues, structured so operators can find the right answer quickly and confidently rather than guessing or forwarding.

03

Put AI behind capable people

AI accelerates the work, but it does not replace the judgment required to do it. DA uses AI for knowledge retrieval, response drafting, conversation summaries, pattern detection, and quality review. Operators use those tools to move faster and stay better informed. The accountability for the answer stays with the person handling the conversation.

04

Give operators context

People should not have to repeatedly explain information the team already has. Before responding, we review the person's previous conversations, account status, and any known issues affecting their situation. Context is not optional. It's the difference between a generic response and a useful one.

05

Investigate before escalating

Operators identify the actual issue before moving it elsewhere. Escalating without investigation wastes the time of the receiving team, keeps the person asking for help waiting longer, and trains operators to avoid problems rather than solve them. When escalation is necessary, it should happen because the issue requires a different capability, not because the operator didn't look first.

06

Escalate intelligently

When another team is required, provide that team with the context necessary to act. Escalations should arrive with a clear problem statement, relevant account information, what the operator already checked, and what specifically is needed. A useful escalation is the beginning of a resolution path. A bad one adds a step to the queue.

07

Close the loop

Important issues and repeated failures should reach the teams that can address them: product, engineering, operations, billing, documentation, or leadership. Support gives us a direct view of what people experience. We turn those conversations into clear feedback the wider team can act on.

08

Continuously improve

Every support interaction should make the operation smarter. That means reviewing quality, tracking recurring issues, updating documentation, refining escalation logic, and adjusting workflows based on what the data is showing. A support operation that looks the same at month six as it did at month one is not being managed. It's just being staffed.

The Problem

People want help, not another reply.

You've already explained the problem, but the next agent asks you to start over. You get a standard reply that doesn't answer your question. You're asked to wait another day, with no explanation of what happens next.

These problems happen when support teams lack the product knowledge, context, or authority to help. Scripts can handle routine questions, but more complicated issues need someone who can investigate and follow through.

Unresolved problems create more work. People contact you again, your team spends time revisiting the same issues, and some people leave without telling you why.

DA Operations is US-based. We learn the products we support and communicate clearly. We investigate problems, explain the next steps, and follow through with the people we help.

What poor support creates

  • Unnecessary escalations
  • Repeat tickets for the same issue
  • Frustrated people who stop trying
  • Wasted internal team time
  • Lost trust and damaged reputation
  • Feedback that never gets used
  • No visibility into what's going wrong

Tools that help us support people.

Support still requires judgment. We remain responsible for understanding the problem, validating the answer, knowing when something requires investigation, and escalating correctly.

The tool can accelerate the work. Someone still has to own the answer.
